## Pangram text detection

This is the default mode and the one the Medium reviewer spent the most time on. They fed it text generated by ChatGPT, Claude and DeepSeek (the last via file upload) and concluded that Pangram "might come close" to accurate detection. The homepage adds that the detector also identifies AI assistance rather than only fully generated text, which the review attributes to Pangram 3.0. If your policy is a simple pass/fail on submitted work, this is the mode you would use, and the pricing page is where the per-plan limits live.

## Pangram image detection

A newer addition, advertised on the homepage with its own upload widget. The constraints are spelled out there: JPG, PNG or WebP, a minimum of 512 by 512 pixels, up to 30 MB per file, and three free scans per day. It answers a narrower question than the text mode - was this picture generated - and is useful when sourcing illustrations or checking submitted photography.

## Pangram Text Comparison and AI Phrase Detection

The Medium review calls AI Phrase Detection "the feature no one talks about enough". Text Comparison puts two pieces next to each other for when one draft feels a little too close to another; Phrase Detection highlights the specific spans that look machine-written instead of returning a single score. For an editor these two are arguably more actionable than the headline verdict, because they show what to send back for rewriting.

## Pangram Gmail integration

Listed as new on the homepage under solutions. The sources give no detail beyond its existence, so treat it as an option for people who triage a lot of inbound email and want the detector where the text arrives rather than in a separate tab.

## No detector: process-based review

Tim Requarth's Substack post argues that AI detection is really AI inference software with real limitations, and that companies which co-author the accuracy research, stir up concern and then sell the remedy have a conflict of interest. His earlier post makes the case that detecting and punishing individual authors is self-defeating. Notably, he added a correction on April 17 2026 saying he got the piece partly wrong after going back to the technical literature, so read both the post and the update. The alternative he points toward is reviewing the work and the process instead of relying on a verdict.

- You need a yes/no on a submitted essay or deliverable: Pangram text detection, and read the pricing page first.
